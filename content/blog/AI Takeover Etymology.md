The exact two-word phrase **"AI takeover"** has no single documented "coiner"—it emerged organically as a compound noun out of late-20th-century science fiction and early-2000s existential risk discourse, replacing older phrases like "machine rebellion," "robot revolt," and "cybernetic revolt". However, the *concept* it names has a clear intellectual lineage, and the exact phrase crystallized into a formal analytical term between 2000 and 2023.

### 1. Conceptual Precursors (Before the Phrase)

Long before "AI" was coined at Dartmouth in 1956, writers and mathematicians articulated the core dynamic of human displacement by autonomous machines:

- **1863 / 1872 — Samuel Butler:** In his 1863 newspaper letter *"Darwin among the Machines"* and the expanded *"Book of the Machines"* in *Erewhon* (1872), Butler argued that machines were undergoing rapid evolutionary selection and would eventually supplant humanity as the dominant species on Earth.
- **1920 — Karel Čapek:** His play *R.U.R. (Rossum's Universal Robots)* coined the word "robot" and established the modern narrative template of artificial workers overthrowing their human creators.
- **1951 — Alan Turing:** In a lecture on *Intelligent Machinery, A Heretical Theory*, Turing warned that once the machine thinking method started, "it would not take long to outstrip our feeble powers," adding that "at some stage therefore we should have to expect the machines to take control."
- **1965 — I.J. Good:** Good formalized the mechanism of an **"intelligence explosion"**—arguing that an "ultraintelligent machine" could design even better machines, leaving human intelligence far behind.
- **1993 — Vernor Vinge:** In his essay *"The Coming Technological Singularity,"* Vinge warned that creating superhuman intelligence would mark the end of the human era.

### 2. Emergence of the Exact Term ("AI Takeover")

- **1980s–1990s (Sci-Fi & Pop Culture):** Following *Colossus: The Forbin Project* (1970), *The Terminator*(1984), and *The Matrix* (1999), media criticism and encyclopedic categorization (including early Wikipedia entries in the mid-2000s) standardized **"AI takeover"** as the umbrella trope name covering Skynet-style military coups, HAL 9000 mutinies, and subterranean machine control.
- **2000–2014 (Formalization in Existential Risk Research):** As Eliezer Yudkowsky (founding MIRI in 2000) and Nick Bostrom (*Existential Risks*, 2002; *Superintelligence*, 2014) moved the topic from fiction into formal decision theory and risk analysis, **"AI takeover"** became the standard shorthand for a specific failure mode: *an advanced AI system permanently wresting decisive strategic control from humanity*.
