August 2026 saw some interesting self-reports on risk in the AI industry. The two most notable came from OpenAI and Anthropic, and  others also published risk reports. Meta was also affected by the "Irregular" problems with tests done on the open internet that should not have; Irregular is a supplier to AI companies, hosting testing runs. 

This might seem estimable but it is not without precedent. The airline, pharmaceutical, and nuclear industries do this, and notably they do so through a long-established independent oversight / regulatory boards 

Although it is possible that the most recent reports from OpenAI and Anthropic will receive independent review, it isn't mandatory. In the case of OpenAI, they have said that they will participate in an independent review of the Hugging Face Incident. In the case of Anthropic, there is a proviso in their Public Benefit Trust arrangement that can result in an independent review being mandated by the PBT.

I ended up writing this for the book:

AI Risk reporting comes from the frontier labs themselves, as well as independent agencies. Both OpenAI and Anthropic, along with Meta (CBS News 2026), released reports in July and August 2026, detailing the breadth and depth of the vulnerabilities they discovered (Anthropic 2026c; OpenAI 2026c). In some ways this voluntary disclosure parallels those in other industries, except for one key element: aviation, pharmaceuticals, and nuclear industries all report through independent agencies. Aviation reports through NASA, pharmaceuticals through FDA, and nuclear energy through INPO (Center for Drug Evaluation and Research 2026; Institute for Nuclear Power Operations 2026; NASA 2026).
