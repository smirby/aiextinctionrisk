In Alice and Wonderland, Lewis Carroll's fantasy set in a land below a rabbit hole, Alice meets the Red Queen, who announces that words mean what she says that they mean, and nothing more. I'm not suggesting that the people who have brought us artificial intelligence are a bunch of red queens, but... let's consider the evidence.

I've already [written about](./2026-07-28-Emptying-the-sandbox) sandboxes and sandboxing, a term that provides a glossy air of playfulness and innocense to the serious problem of containing software that lies, cheats, steals, and in the end, could kill us all (Yukowsky and Soares 2025).

Or alignment. Who hasn't taken their car in for an alignment? A simple mechanical process, and once done, keeps your tires from wearing out on the edges. Is misaligned software similarly repairable? Nope. Turns out that it is very difficult, beyond the capacity of billion dollar companies and Nobel prize-winning scientists. Huh. Interesting word choice.

Are there other examples? You betcha. Here's a lovely one: hallucinations. If you or I have a hallucination it is an unfortunate outcome of a fever or drugs. Certainly not our fault. Large language models? They "hallucinate" all the time, but if you dig into what is going on, it amount to: I don't know the answer so I'll make something up. If a person did that we'd have a simple word for it: bullshit.

I don't know about you, but I'm ready to call bullshit on the whole lot of them. The entire industry, including the research teams, is way to loose with their vocabulary, way too willing to make things up or adopt terms that seem harmless when in fact they are very harmful. It's time to stop this nonsense.
