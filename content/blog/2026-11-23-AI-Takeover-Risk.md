The AI Takeover Risk and Canada’s Response

William Leiss and Richard Smith: 23 November 2026

Paul Christiano, who delivered the three 2026 Hinton Lectures (available on YouTube) earlier this month, is one of the world’s foremost authorities on AI safety research. He recently predicted that there is a significant chance of an AI “takeover” within the next twelve months. This means that an AI system will hack and then take control over a major institution, corporate entity, or even a government, somewhere in the world.

Canada’s federal government has had a policy to follow the precautionary principle for a long time, which commits it to taking cost-effective action against an imminent, realistic, and very serious threat from any technology *before* it materializes and begins to cause major and irreversible damage.

In this light we strongly recommend that the Government of Canada should use its emergency powers now, in late 2026, to require designated Canadian institutions to develop, and demonstrate in advance the efficacy of, the capacity to disconnect fully their operating systems from the Internet at a moment’s notice. The top tier of such institutions is: all of the major banks; all operators of electricity systems and water treatment facilities in all provinces; all police and RCMP units; the Canadian Forces; and others to be named.

In addition to the severing of Internet connectivity, other measures may be required in order to secure an institution’s intranet, telephone systems, and use of AI applications from external threats. Each designated institution shall be required to have a dedicated team, available at all times 24/7, capable of implementing a variety of shutdown orders at once.

This plan should be coordinated by a special team in the Prime Minister’s office, which should have a full-time dedicated staff operating continuously on a 24/7 basis. This office should have a mandate to (a) perform an ongoing threat analysis of global developments relevant to the risk of an AI takeover scenario, and (b) provide expert guidance on preparatory response measures to all potentially-affected institutions.

**William Leiss and Richard Smith are the authors of \*Treacherous Tech: Taking the Risk of Artificial Intelligence Seriously\*, forthcoming 2027 from McGill-Queen’s University Press**
